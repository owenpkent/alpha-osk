# Taking part in the Alpha-OSK typing study

**Consent form version 0.1, 2026-09-07.**

Please read this before you decide. There is no rush, and you can close it and
come back. Nothing is recorded until you press the button at the end.

If it is easier for someone to read this with you, that is completely fine.

---

## What this is

Alpha-OSK tries to save you clicks by guessing the word you are typing. In
testing against text on a computer, it avoids about half of them.

That test assumes a person who spots every suggestion the moment it appears and
never misses. Real people are not like that. Reading a suggestion takes time.
Moving to it takes time. Sometimes it is the wrong word.

**Nobody knows how much of that half is real.** That is the only thing this
study is trying to find out.

## What you would be asked to do

You copy short phrases that we give you, on your own computer, at home, in your
own time. There is no video call. Nobody watches you.

- It takes about **25 minutes**, and you can **split it across days**.
- You do it twice: once with the word suggestions **on**, once with them
  **off**. Which comes first is decided at random.
- There is a practice round each time, which does not count.
- After each round, six short questions about how hard it felt.
- You can take a break whenever you want. There is no timer.

**You do not need to be good at this.** We are measuring the software, not you.
There is no score and nothing to pass.

## What we record

Only while you are copying one of our phrases:

- the phrase we gave you, and what you actually typed
- when each click happened, so we can work out speed
- which suggestions were offered and which ones you took
- your answers to the six questions

We also record what kind of pointing device you use and roughly how long you
have used an on-screen keyboard.

## What we never record

- **Anything you type outside the task.** Recording starts when a phrase
  appears and stops when you finish it.
- **Your name, email, address, or date of birth.** We never ask.
- Your diagnosis. We do not ask and we do not need it.
- Your IP address, your files, your screen, or which other programs are open.

You are identified only by a random code like `P-4f2a91c3`, made on your own
computer. It is not built from anything about you or your machine. We cannot
work out who you are from it, and neither can anyone else.

## Nothing is sent until you say so

When you finish, **you see your own results first**: how fast you typed, how
many clicks the suggestions saved you, and how that compares to the 50% figure
above. It is your result and you get to see it.

Then you decide whether to send it. If you do not, it stays on your computer.

You can also open the file yourself and read it before sending. It is plain
text.

## Stopping and changing your mind

- **You can stop at any point**, from any screen, without giving a reason.
- If you stop, you are asked whether to delete what has been collected. If you
  say yes, it is deleted from your computer straight away.
- **After sending, you can still have it deleted.** Email the address below with
  your random code. You do not have to explain why.
- Your prediction settings and your keyboard's learned vocabulary are **not
  changed** by taking part, and are never sent.

## Risks and benefits

**Risk: tiredness.** Copying phrases with a pointer is tiring, and for many
people reading this, fatigue is not a small thing. That is why it is 25 minutes,
why the breaks have no timer, and why you can finish another day. Please stop if
you are tired. Stopping early is genuinely fine and the data is still useful.

There is no physical risk. There is no financial risk.

**Benefit: none to you directly.** You will not type faster because you took
part. You will see your own numbers, which some people find interesting.

The point is that assistive keyboards are mostly tested by the people who wrote
them, on themselves. This one currently has one long-term user, who wrote it. A
result from real people changes what gets built next, and because the data and
the analysis are published, it is useful to other people building these tools
too.

**You are not being paid.** Nobody is being paid, so nobody is taking part
because they feel they need the money.

## Who is running this, and an important limitation

This study is run by **Owen Kent**, who wrote Alpha-OSK. He uses it every day as
his own main way of typing, because he has muscular dystrophy.

**There is no university behind this, and no ethics committee has reviewed
it.** You should know that before you decide. Normally a study like this is
checked in advance by an independent board. This one has not been.

What is in place instead:

- The full plan was **published before anyone took part**, so the results cannot
  be quietly reinterpreted afterwards. It is at
  `docs/research/STUDY_PROTOCOL.md` in the project's public code.
- **The data and the analysis will be published**, so anyone can check the
  result, including a result that makes the keyboard look bad.
- The information collected is deliberately kept thin enough that losing it
  would not harm anyone.

That is not the same as an ethics review, and it is not offered as if it were.
It is what is honestly available, and you should weigh it.

## What happens to the results

Anonymous data from everyone who takes part is combined and published, together
with the analysis, in a public repository. It may be written up in a paper.

**Nothing published will identify you.** If you write something in the optional
comment box that could identify you, it is removed before publication.
